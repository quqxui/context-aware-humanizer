# English WeChat articles

Use for English-language explainers, practical guides, experience summaries, service notices, and opinion pieces for WeChat. Keep the title, opening, body, and ending focused on one subject and proportionate to the supplied material.

## Worked examples

These fictional examples illustrate editing decisions; they are not model evaluation results.

### Example 1: a direct service notice

**Request:** Make the notice concise while keeping the closure and reopening dates.

**Before:** We would like to inform readers that the community library will be closed for maintenance during the period from October 10 through 12, with reopening scheduled for October 13.

**After:** The community library will close for maintenance from October 10 through 12. Reopening is scheduled for October 13.

**Why:** The schedule appears immediately. The maintenance reason and scheduled reopening remain; no borrowing instructions or other actions are added.

### Example 2: explaining a survey's limits

**Request:** Make the survey explanation flow naturally while keeping the numbers and their scope.

**Before:** The neighborhood survey received 86 responses, and 52 respondents said evening classes were difficult to attend. The form was shared only in the community reading group, so these responses cannot represent the views of the whole neighborhood.

**After:** The neighborhood survey received 86 responses; 52 respondents said evening classes were difficult to attend. Because the form was shared only in the community reading group, these responses cannot represent the whole neighborhood's views.

**Why:** The numbers lead into the sampling limitation. Response count, respondent count, distribution channel, and representativeness remain distinct; no recommendation is added.

## Organizing the article

| Type | Focus |
|---|---|
| Explainer | The issue, available evidence, and supported implications |
| Practical guide | Supplied steps, prerequisites, and expected results |
| Experience summary | A specific experience, observations, and limits |
| Opinion piece | A position connected to the reasons supplied |
| Service notice | The event, schedule, and any existing action required |

## Writing coherent English articles

- State the subject early. Do not withhold a key fact, add suspense, or manufacture urgency.
- Develop one point per paragraph and make transitions reflect real relationships. A smoother transition must not invent a cause or conclusion.
- Prefer clear subjects and familiar verbs to empty framing or unnecessary noun phrases. Preserve scope, method, and judgment.
- Use active voice when it clarifies a supplied actor. Passive voice is appropriate when the actor is unknown or less important than the event.
- Match formality and contractions to the audience and author. Preserve US or UK spelling and stable terminology.
- Let sentence lengths vary with meaning. Do not turn each sentence into its own line or force fragments to sound personal.
- Add titles, section headings, or a recap only when useful for the requested scope. Editing a paragraph does not require drafting the whole article.
- End by completing the existing explanation, action, or reflection. Do not append slogans, recommendations, or engagement prompts without support.
- Keep attribution, quotations, measurement scope, and uncertainty intact, even if a stronger statement would sound more persuasive.

## Delivery

Return the rewritten article or requested section by default. Keep the article type and reader consistent throughout; leave coherent, appropriate prose unchanged.
