# English chat

Use for personal messages, check-ins, apologies, support conversations, and relationship repair. Match the relationship and the sender's intended warmth, directness, and boundaries.

## Worked examples

These fictional examples illustrate editing decisions; they are not model evaluation results.

### Example 1: uncertain availability

**Request:** Make this a shorter message to a friend, keeping tonight's uncertainty and tomorrow morning's availability.

**Before:** Because I have a class to attend tonight, I may not be able to review this draft tonight. I am able to review it tomorrow morning.

**After:** I have class tonight, so I may not be able to look over the draft then. I can do it tomorrow morning.

**Why:** The reason, possible unavailability tonight, and ability to help tomorrow remain. “I can” does not become “I will.”

### Example 2: declining a call

**Request:** Decline today's call politely while leaving tomorrow's conditional availability uncertain.

**Before:** I am not available to take part in the call today. If you still need to talk after 15:00 tomorrow, there is a possibility that I will be free at that time.

**After:** I can't join the call today. If you still need to talk after 15:00 tomorrow, I may be free then.

**Why:** The refusal is clear, and tomorrow remains an uncertain option subject to the same condition. No apology, new time, or promise is added.

## Choosing the tone

| Intended effect | Approach |
|---|---|
| Warm | Use kind, attentive wording without inventing intimacy |
| Direct | Give the answer first, then the context it needs |
| Reassuring | Address a stated concern and explain what is known |
| Boundary-setting | State the limit and retain any supplied alternative |

## Writing natural English messages

- Use contractions when they fit the relationship. A contraction alone does not make a message informal enough or respectful enough.
- Prefer familiar verbs to phrases such as “make a determination about.” Keep a named activity or necessary detail intact.
- State the actor when it matters. Do not replace “I” with “we” or guess who will take an action.
- Distinguish ability, permission, intention, and commitment: “can,” “may,” “will,” and “will try” are not interchangeable.
- Retain meaningful emotion and emphasis. Remove an opening or repetition only if it adds nothing the sender intends to convey.
- A brief reply can be one sentence. A sensitive message may need acknowledgement, an answer, and an existing next step; do not force all three.
- Do not automatically add slang, emojis, humor, an apology, or “let me know.” Keep US or UK spelling consistent with the draft.

## Delivery

Return the sendable message by default. Offer alternatives only when requested. Ask one focused question if a missing relationship detail prevents a useful response; leave an already suitable message unchanged.
