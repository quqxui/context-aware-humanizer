# English X posts

Use for a single X post, a thread, or a reply. Establish the requested unit of output before changing length, order, or presentation.

## Worked examples

These fictional examples illustrate editing decisions; they are not model evaluation results.

### Example 1: a direct release update

**Request:** Produce one publishable update, retaining the release date and repository detail.

**Before:** We would like to share the news that our team released an open-source parser on Monday and included examples in the repository.

**After:** Our team open-sourced a parser on Monday. Examples are in the repository.

**Why:** The empty opening is removed. The team, Monday release, open-source status, and examples remain, without a new invitation or performance claim.

### Example 2: a two-post thread with evidence limits

**Request:** Make this a two-post thread while retaining the benchmark setup, source, and conditional plan to test it.

**Before:** I read the maintainers' release notes for the export update. Their benchmark used a 10 GB test file and reports exports 30% faster after a change to the caching path. I haven't run that benchmark or tested the new version on our logs. The result is useful for deciding whether to try the update, but it doesn't show that every project will see a 30% gain. If I have time next week, I'll run a comparison on our logs; for now, I wouldn't call the speedup verified in our setting.

**After:**

> 1/2 I read the maintainers' release notes: their 10 GB benchmark reports 30% faster exports after a caching change. I haven't run their benchmark.
>
> 2/2 I haven't tested the update on our logs either. The result is a reason to try it, not a verified speedup for our setting or a guarantee for every project. If I have time next week, I'll compare it on our logs.

**Why:** The first post states what the maintainers measured; the second separates our unverified setting and conditional next step. The 10 GB test file, reported 30%, and limits remain explicit.

## Organizing the format

| Format | Approach |
|---|---|
| Single post | One self-contained claim or update |
| Thread | Necessary context first, then an ordered argument |
| Reply | Engage with the prior post's actual point |
| Community update | Explain what happened and what readers need to know |

## Writing English for X

- Put the central point early. Remove generic openings only if they do not convey an intended attitude or judgment.
- Compression must retain source, negation, conditions, uncertainty, dates, links, and quoted wording.
- Follow the user's length constraint. Do not assume a platform character limit, required hashtag, or mandatory call to action.
- Use precise, familiar verbs and contractions when appropriate. Keep spelling variety and technical names stable; do not force slang or sentence fragments.
- In a thread, make each unit understandable while preserving the argument's order. Use numbering when it helps the requested sequence.
- Preserve the strength of an opinion or criticism. Do not manufacture outrage, confidence, audience agreement, or promised engagement.
- Avoid automatic closing questions, recommendations, or requests to repost. Short sentences and varied rhythm are options, not targets.

## Delivery

Return the requested post, thread, or reply by default. Respect a local editing scope; do not expand a single update into a thread unless requested. Leave an already suitable draft unchanged.
